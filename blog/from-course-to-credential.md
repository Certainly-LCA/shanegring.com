# From Course to Credential: What We’re Learning About Go-to-Market Strategy

The work didn't change. The positioning did.

The work didn’t change. The positioning did.

If you're not building a certification

Written about selling certifications. The lesson is wider than that. People were not asking for a website, they were asking what to do next. Leading with what someone walks away with, rather than what gets built, changed the conversation. It is why the offers on this site are named the way they are: [a score, an opinion, a plan, the build](https://shanegring.com/work-with-me).

A few weeks ago, I was speaking with someone who has taught hundreds of people how to build online courses. And she said something that’s stuck with me:

*“They keep coming back, asking what to do next. I don’t know what to tell them.”*

For many of her clients, the initial success is there. They’ve built a course, delivered it to a few cohorts, and maybe even made it profitable.

But eventually, the question shows up:** **

**What now?**

## The next step isn’t always obvious

Some try to launch another course. Others explore books, cohorts, or masterminds.

But what this educator kept hearing (and feeling herself) was a desire to **solidify what they’d built:**

→ To give their method a little more structure.
→ A little more recognition.
→ A little more staying power.

And for a long time, she had the same instinct I did:

*“Maybe what they need isn't something new, but a clear next step.”*

## Building the product before the pitch

At some point, I realized I wasn’t just helping her shape what came next - I was relearning something about my own work.

It reminded me of something I’d forgotten:

It’s easy to get stuck polishing the idea in your own head.
Harder, but more valuable, to refine it in dialogue with your customers.

That’s what’s been happening. And that’s what started to **reshape how I think about our own go-to-market.**

## Rethinking the GTM stack

Historically, we’d lead with services: brand, website, content, campaigns. But the more we talked to people interested in exploring a "next step" in their business, the more we realized:

They weren’t asking for a website.

**They were asking for direction:**

*→ “How do I structure this?” *

*→ “How does this fit into what I’ve already built?” *

*→ “How does this become a new revenue stream for my business?”*

The work didn't change, but what we led with did.

This is why we built a playbook, not just for delivery, but for **go-to-market.**

We’d help define the certification itself, but also offer the services for:

- Messaging and positioning

- Website and landing page copy

- Launch plan

- Optional paid ad strategy (if they’re ready)

All of this has been offered upfront, not as marketing for their business, but as **a new business **
*
**line**
*
** in itself.**

And that’s created a very different kind of conversation.

## When the positioning finally clicked

We didn't reinvent what we do, but we did get clearer on what people were actually buying.

And as a founder, I’ve been re-learning something I thought I already knew:

People don’t really want marketing.
They want forward motion.

Most go-to-market strategies assume that the customer already **knows **what they want next.

In reality, many of the people you’re talking to **don’t**, and that's not a failure - it's a natural consequence of success.

So when your customer is asking:

*“What is the most sensible next move, given what I’ve already built?”*

... your GTM has to answer that.

**At the end of the day, this isn't just about certifications. It's about what happens when you give people structure for:**

- Their ideas

- Their IP

- Their revenue and go-to market strategy

That’s why this model is starting to click.

You won't only be selling another service.

You'll be giving people a next step that makes sense for them and aligns with what their audience is already asking for.

Onward into 2026,
