# Your website is a product, not a poster

Certification websites need to think about what they do, not how they look.

Certification websites need to think about what they do, not how they look.

Many certification organizations I meet treat their sites like a poster.

They are places to announce or describe what they do. It looks good, but serves little purpose.

Yet in 2025, most people have their first and most meaningful interaction with your program through the website. It’s where they apply, find standards and templates, confirm credentials, and return to stay engaged.

## The website has become the product itself:

**→ It is measurable. **Products have metrics. You can track how many users complete an application, where they drop off, and which resources are most used. When you have this data, you can make improvements for better results.

**→ It is iterative. **Products evolve. Your standards and your systems change, so your site should as well. The most successful organizations don’t redesign every five years; they make small, regular improvements each quarter.

**→ It is integrated.** Your website should connect to your CRM, certification portal, and community systems. When those pieces work together, your team stops repeating work and starts learning from its data because it can see how visitors travel from the first visit to working with you.

When you shift to looking at your website as a product, you can focus on **its goal.**

The strongest certification organizations are quietly building **trust infrastructure: **digital systems that reflect the credibility of their brand through how usable and connected they are.

They don’t have inexplicable drop-offs between applications and submissions - they know what happened to the users, and how they can make it better in the next iteration cycle.

**So when a website doesn’t deliver, it’s not a lack of effort.** In most cases, it simply means it reflects an old set of goals or plays by old rules.

Once the purpose is clear, the design naturally finds its direction. And when digital is treated as a product, it becomes an investment in the mission itself rather than just the promotion of pretty words.

**→ If you had to pick one thing your website should do better today, what would it be?**
