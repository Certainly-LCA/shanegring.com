# The Certification Readiness Test: 10 Questions to Know If Your IP Can Carry a Credential

Before you build anything, answer these 10 questions.

Before you build anything, answer these 10 questions.

If you're not building a certification

Written for people deciding whether to turn their method into a credential. The move underneath it works anywhere: before you build the thing, write down what has to be true for it to pay off, then answer honestly. That is the same test I run on a site before an [Install](https://shanegring.com/install).

When people come to us thinking about turning their work into a certification, we don’t start with platforms or badges.

We start with **fit**.

Because the biggest trap I see is when someone thinks a certification is just a fancier course. It’s **not**. A certification is proof that you finished something, a public standard, and a longer-term initiative that often includes renewals and community.

Over the years, we’ve identified **10 major questions that predict whether a certification is likely to work** for your business (and whether the investment makes sense).

So before you build anything, here’s the readiness test we use:

## The 10-question readiness test

Usually, we look at four buckets:

- IP

- Demand

- Business-model fit

- Brand readiness

And in practice, you can use the following 10 questions to determine **where you currently are** on the readiness scale.

1️⃣ **Do you have a repeatable IP? **

Is your “method” something you can teach through a course, workshop, book, or process, so it can be applied consistently?

2️⃣ **Is there a clear market demand for what you do? **

Not “it’s interesting,” but a clear pull for the product or service your IP supports.

3️⃣ **Are clients asking for higher levels of training?**
A signal that the market wants “deeper mastery,” not just an intro.

4️⃣ **Do you have an audience list?**
Because a credential needs a reachable, committed people to launch to.

5️⃣ **Do you have a social following? **

Same idea: do you have an existing channel where people already pay attention?

6️⃣ **Does your work have enough recognition that people would want to associate with it?**

In other words, does affiliating with your brand feel valuable to your audience?

7️⃣ **Do you actually want to introduce this into your business stack?**
A certification isn’t just a content project - it becomes part of how your business operates.

8️⃣ **Does scalable recurring revenue make sense for your model? **

Versus relying mostly on one-off course sales or one-on-one work.

9️⃣** Is your brand strong enough to diffuse through other people? **

Meaning, are you ready for other individuals and groups to represent your brand moving forward by getting your certification?

🔟 **Are you ready to introduce that into the mix? **

Brand diffusion changes everything - support, governance, consistency, expectations. Are you ready for that responsibility?

## A simple way to interpret your score

If you’re strong on **IP + demand**, you can build something.

If you’re strong on **brand + audience**, you can probably launch something.

But if you’re not ready for renewals + community + ongoing engagement, what you’re building may behave more like a course than a certification - and the market will treat it that way.
