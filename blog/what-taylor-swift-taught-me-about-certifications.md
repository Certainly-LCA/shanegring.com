# What Taylor Swift taught me about certifications

My wife's a Swiftie. The loyalty structure isn't that different from what we're building.

If you're not building a certification

Written about credential loyalty. The part that travels: people stay with something they can call themselves part of and keep taking part in. That is a question about what your systems let someone do, join, and come back to, not about what your copy says. See [the Install](https://shanegring.com/install).

My wife is a massive Taylor Swift fan.

We've traveled to Portugal, Toronto, Miami, and Indianapolis. Watching her engage with that community has been great, honestly, and I've been thinking about why the Taylor Swift phenomenon works so well as a business.

There are some uncomfortable parallels to certification that I can't ignore.

Stay with me here:

What Taylor and certifications both do: they create identity alignment.

When my wife identifies as a Swiftie, she's aligning herself with values: authenticity, storytelling, resilience, and community. The music is the vehicle, but *
**the identity**
* is what creates loyalty.

**Certification works the same way.** When someone becomes certified in your methodology, they're aligning their professional identity with it.

They're publicly stating: *"This is how I do my work. This is what I believe in."*

That alignment creates loyalty that course completion never could.

## Parallel #1. The recurring revenue structure

🎵 **Taylor has:** concerts, albums, re-releases, merch drops, and vault tracks.
🎯 **Certifications have:** applications, exams, continuing ed, renewals, and events.

Both models create **ongoing participation**. Swifties stay engaged. Same with credential holders; they're invested in maintaining their standing over time.

## Parallel #2. The community component

Swifties have their bracelet exchanges, concert meetups, and online groups. There's shared language and inside jokes.

It's tribal, and it creates belonging.

Certifications do this through cohorts, directories, annual events, and community forums. Credential holders identify with each other. They reference shared training experiences and help each other navigate the standard.

**The tribe forms naturally because everyone's invested in the same system.**

## Parallel #3. Markers of allegiance

Taylor has these clear signals of participation: the number of concerts you’ve been to, whether you have the vinyls, and whether you have the limited edition.

There are levels showing how invested you are.

**Certifications have similar markers of allegiance: **

→ Which version of the standard did you train under?
→ Have you recertified?
→ Are you in the directory?
→ Do you have an advanced credential?

Each marker signals depth of commitment.

## Parallel #4. The expansion

Taylor started in country, moved to pop, went into filmmaking, and influenced fashion. The brand expands into adjacent spaces while keeping the core identity intact. Swifties follow because they **trust her instincts** and want to be part of whatever's next.

**Certifications evolve similarly.**

The core methodology stays consistent, but you add advanced credentials, specialized applications, and partnerships.

The ecosystem expands around the original standard. Practitioners follow because they're already invested and trust where you're taking it.

**I'm not suggesting certifications need to become fandoms. The loyalty mechanics are worth studying, though.**

When you build a system that:

- Lets people align their identity with your work

- Gives them recurring reasons to participate

- Creates community around shared values

- Offers clear markers of belonging

...you are inviting them into a long-term relationship.

**That's what creates lasting authority: a structure people want to belong to.**

**DRVN Golf** is seeing this unfold with their network. Their trainers identify with the methodology rather than simply using it, putting it on their websites and referencing it throughout their client conversations.

They want to be known as *"the trainer who uses the DRVN system."*

Taylor Swift is running a certification model. (She probably doesn't think about it that way, but the structure is there.)

And if it works for building one of the most loyal fan bases in the world, maybe there's something here worth learning from.

*→ Have you seen any brands or methodologies that create this kind of loyalty in your space?*
