# Want to build a certification that actually matters?

Anyone can slap a badge on a site. But trust? That takes more.

Anyone can slap a badge on a site. But trust? That takes more.

If you're not building a certification

Written about credentials, but the four levers hold for any claim a business makes: a standard people can read, requirements that are not a secret, review that keeps happening, and a market that asks for it. Your website decides whether the first two are true. That is most of what [the Read](https://shanegring.com/read) looks at.

Anyone can slap a badge on a website.

But real certification? That’s about trust. And trust is earned.

In every project I’ve worked on, from LEED to WELL to the new programs we’re helping launch today, the same four credibility levers always show up.

## Here are the four credibility levers:

1️⃣ *A Clear, Public Standard *

No gatekeeping. No mystery. Just visible, auditable expectations.

2️⃣ *Transparent Requirements *

So anyone (not just insiders) can understand what it takes to pass.

3️⃣ *Ongoing Evaluation *

Certification shouldn’t be one-and-done. It has to evolve with science, new information, or evolving priorities.

4️⃣ *Market Recognition *

If no one asks for it, it won’t last. The market has to care.

That’s what separates a certification from a course. It’s what moves it from content… to currency.

If you’re building a certification, these four pillars should show up in every slide deck, every system, every strategy.

I once helped lead a standard that scaled to 100+ countries, and it started by getting these four things right.

So let me ask: 👉 What do you think your market values most in a credential?

Reply and let me know.
