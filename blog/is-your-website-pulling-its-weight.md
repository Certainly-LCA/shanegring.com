# Is your website pulling its weight?

If your certification site isn't helping people take action, it's not doing enough.

If your certification site isn’t helping people take action, it’s not doing enough.

There’s a pattern I’ve noticed in certification orgs: They treat their website like a brochure, when it should be working more like a control panel.

When you run a certification program, your site isn’t just “marketing.” It’s the place where applicants start their journey, project teams download tools and templates, credential holders check deadlines, partners access brand assets, staff send people for info, etc.

It’s way more than a brochure these days: it’s a critical system.

When we ignore that, we end up with beautiful sites that don’t actually *do* much and pages that look great, but don’t support the people we serve.

## Before you design a page, we recommend starting with these questions:

🔸**What actions should this enable?**

Can someone start an application without emailing your staff? Can they download what they need without digging through six tabs?* *

*If not, your site is adding friction, not solving it.*

🔸**What decisions should it support?**

Your users need to know: Is this right for us? Are we eligible? What’s it going to take?

*Your site should answer those questions clearly, without a 30-minute webinar or a PDF maze.*

🔸**What systems does it need to connect to?**

Marketing sites can’t be siloed. They should pull in updates from your standard, link to your certification portal, integrate with your CRM.

*Otherwise, you’re doing double entry. Or worse, leaving gaps in the experience.*

When you ask these questions first, the design has a direction. → Form follows function. → Strategy leads the build.

Because in this work, your site shouldn’t just reflect your program, it should help you run it.

If your site isn’t doing what you need it to, it’s not a failure. It just means it was built for a different job. The good news: That’s always fixable.
