Nobody owns the digital side of your business.

Not marketing, not IT, and not the agency that built the site three years ago. It falls to you, on top of everything else, until it stops getting done altogether. Here is what to do about it, depending on how much of it you want to keep holding.

What do you want to walk away with?

Four depths, in order. Each one is a thing you keep, and each credits in full toward the next.

a score The Scan What AI engines can read about you, scored. It commits you to nothing. Free · 2 minutes an opinion The Read A person watches your site the way a buyer and a machine both do, and ranks every fix. $450 · 2 days a plan The Map How your business actually runs, on paper, with a fixed-price build proposal as the last page. $1,999 · 3 weeks the thing built The Site Your website rebuilt as the operating surface of the business. Custom · 6 to 12 weeks

The order is the point. A score tells you whether to look, an opinion tells you what is wrong, a plan tells you what to build, and the build is the last step rather than the first.

When none of it is the answer

Four situations where I would tell you to keep your money. They come up often enough to be worth putting on the page.

  • What you actually want is a visual refresh of the site you have
  • The real problem is demand, not operations
  • Nobody inside the business thinks anything needs to change
  • You want the diagnosis, but you do not intend to act on it

Told apart in one line each.

Autopilot vs. Install

The Autopilot means I do it. The Install means you can. Same underlying work, opposite answer to who's holding the keyboard in six months.

Read vs. Map

The Read is my opinion on your site, in five days. The Map is your business extracted onto paper, in three weeks, and it's where every build starts.

Autopilot vs. Seat

The Autopilot owns your website. The Seat owns your operations. One is a surface, one is a seat.
